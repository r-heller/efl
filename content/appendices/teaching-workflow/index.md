The repeating cycle behind every Unit on this site, from
Bildungsplan to feedback.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  A[Lehrplan-Abgleich<br/>curriculum framework ("Bildungsplan") + Schuljahr] --> B[Unit-Plan<br/>Lernziele, Skills, Exam-Typ]
  B --> C[Activate<br/>Vorwissen, Hook]
  C --> D[Input<br/>Text, Hörverstehen, Grammatik]
  D --> E[Practise<br/>Geleitete Übungen + Antwortkey]
  E --> F[Produce<br/>Schreiben, Sprechen, mediation ("Mediation")]
  F --> G[Reflect<br/>Selbsteinschätzung, Can-do]
  G --> H[Leistungserhebung<br/>class test ("Klassenarbeit") oder school-leaving examination ("Abitur")-Aufgabe]
  H --> I[Feedback<br/>kompetenzbezogen, individuell]
  I --> J[Folgeunit<br/>nächstes Kompetenzfeld]
  J --> A
```

## Notes for teachers

- **Lehrplan-Abgleich** happens once per Schuljahr per year group ("Klassenstufe")
  — open this site's [curriculum framework ("Bildungsplan") page](/bildungsplan/) and
  pick which Kompetenzen each Unit will cover. Every Unit's front
  matter declares its alignment, so the mapping is auditable.
- **Activate → Reflect** is the within-Unit five-step. Each step
  has its own slide(s) in the Unit's slide deck and its own subsection
  in the Unit article. Decks and worksheets ship as committed PDFs, so what
  is shown in class and what is downloaded cannot drift apart.
- **Leistungserhebung** is the worked exam example at the end of
  the Unit, scaled to the Niveau (Klassenarbeit Kl. 5–10 / school-leaving examination ("Abitur")-
  Aufgabe Kl. 11–13).
- **Feedback** is intentionally separate from grading — every Unit
  ships a competence-aligned rubric so feedback can be returned in
  Kompetenzbegriffen, not just as a single Note.
- **Folgeunit** loops back via the recurring cast: characters
  introduced in Unit 1 reappear in later Units of the same
  Klassenstufe to give learners a continuous narrative to lean on.

## Planning rhythm

A Unit is written for roughly three teaching weeks, which in most timetables
means eight to eleven contact hours. That is an estimate, not a
prescription: the five steps are not five lessons, and Activate is often
fifteen minutes rather than a period of its own. Where a group needs longer,
the step to stretch is Practise — it is the only one that tolerates
repetition without losing its point.

## Adapting a Unit

The commonest adaptation is dropping the Apply task and keeping the exam
example. That is a false economy. Apply is where the language leaves the
worksheet, and the exam example is calibrated against it; without the task,
the exam measures recall rather than use.

The second commonest is running the deck without the worksheet. The deck is
the teacher's spine and the worksheet is the learner's record — a group that
leaves with nothing written down has nothing to revise from.
